Overview

DeWalt sells a battery-to-wire adapter that takes a 20V MAX pack and breaks its terminals out into two leads, so the pack can run any DC load. This project adds a 3D printed housing on top of that adapter. Inside the housing, a microcontroller watches the pack through current-sense shunts and drives three regulated outputs: a fixed 12V rail, a fixed 5V rail and an adjustable rail.

  • Input: DeWalt 20V MAX pack (18V nominal, roughly 15–20V across a discharge)
  • Outputs: 12V, 5V and an adjustable rail, plus raw pack voltage
  • Monitoring: per-rail current and pack voltage, read by the microcontroller
  • Enclosure: printed shell that clips onto the existing adapter

Status: design in progress. Values below are targets, not measured results.

All three rails are bucks, since every output sits below the pack voltage. The 12V rail needs enough headroom that it stays in regulation as the pack sags toward its cutoff.

Shunt current sensing

Each rail and the main pack line carries a low-value shunt resistor. The voltage across the shunt is proportional to current (V = I × R), and a current-sense amplifier or monitor IC turns that small drop into something the microcontroller can read.

  • Placement: one shunt on the pack input, one on each rail output.
  • Shunt value: chosen so full-scale current gives a usable drop without wasting much power (P = I² × R). Values TBD.
  • Readout: pack voltage and per-rail current, used for a fuel gauge, overcurrent cutoff and logging.
